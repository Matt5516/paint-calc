How to figure out how much paint to buy

Short answer: multiply the paintable wall area by the number of coats, divide by the coverage on the can (about 350 sq ft per gallon), then buy the cheapest cans that cover it. Most people skip that last step and overpay.

The formula

Wall area = perimeter × height − doors − windows Gallons = area × coats ÷ coverage per gallon Buy = cheapest mix of 5-gallon buckets, gallons and quarts that covers it

Worked example: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls

StepResult
Gross wall area: 2 × (12 + 12) × 8384 sq ft
Minus 1 door (20) and 2 windows (15 each)334 sq ft
Two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon334 × 2 ÷ 350 = 1.91 gallons

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need?

Wall area (perimeter × height, minus doors and windows) × coats ÷ coverage per gallon. Example: a 12 × 12 ft room with 8 ft walls, one door and two windows has 334 sq ft of wall, so two coats at 350 sq ft per gallon is 1.91 gallons: buy 2 gallons.

How much does a gallon of paint cover?

Most cans say 300 to 400 square feet per gallon, and PaintCare suggests using 350 for a rough estimate. Rough textures can need up to 50 percent more paint. A quart covers a quarter of that.

How many gallons of paint for a 12x12 room?

About 2 gallons for the walls with two coats (1.91 gallons exact), and 1 more gallon if you also paint the ceiling.

Is a 5-gallon bucket cheaper than gallons?

Often. At typical 2026 prices ($45 a gallon, $200 a bucket) a bucket costs the same as four gallons plus a quart, so once you need more than 4 gallons the bucket gives you 5 for the price of 4 and a quart. The calculator does that comparison for you.

Do I need primer?

Not always. New drywall, stains, glossy surfaces and big color changes usually do. Add a primer line in the calculator to see the cans and cost.
