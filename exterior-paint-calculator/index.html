The short answer

A 40 × 30 ft one-story house with two 30 ft wide, 8 ft rise gables, 2 doors and 10 windows has 1,450 sq ft to paint. Two coats of smooth siding at 350 sq ft per gallon need 8.29 gallons: 1 five-gallon bucket + 3 gallons + 2 quarts, about $375.

Siding coverage presets

  • Smooth siding: 350 sq ft per gallon
  • Wood clapboard: 300
  • Rough cedar: 250
  • Stucco or brick: 200

These are planning starting points: rough, porous surfaces soak up more paint, and PaintCare notes rough textures can need up to 50 percent more. Check your can’s label.

We use 10 ft per story as a simple rule. For tall first floors or a raised foundation, type a custom wall height.

FAQ

How much paint do I need to paint a house exterior?

Find the wall area (perimeter × height), add gables, subtract doors and windows, multiply by coats and divide by coverage. A 40 × 30 ft one-story house with gables is about 1,450 sq ft, or 8.3 gallons for two coats.

How do I figure gables?

Each gable is a triangle: ½ × width × rise. Add it to the wall area.

Is it cheaper to buy 5-gallon buckets?

At the default prices a bucket ($200) costs the same as four gallons plus a quart ($180 + $20) and holds a full 5 gallons, so past 4 gallons the bucket wins. The calculator picks the cheapest mix for you.

Do I need primer outside?

Bare wood, patched areas and big color changes usually do. Toggle primer to add it.
