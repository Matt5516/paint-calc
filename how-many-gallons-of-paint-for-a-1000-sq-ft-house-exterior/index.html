How many gallons of paint for a 1,000 sq ft house exterior?

Floor area is not wall area: here is the conversion, then the cans.

A 1,000 sq ft one-story house has about 1,075 sq ft of siding to paint and needs 6.14 gallons for two coats of smooth siding (1 five-gallon bucket + 1 gallon + 1 quart, about $265). As a two-story it needs 9.13 gallons (2 five-gallon buckets).

How the floor area becomes wall area

Assumptions: a square footprint (one story: 31.6 ft per side, 126.5 ft around; two stories: 22.4 ft per side, 89.4 ft around), 10 ft of wall per story, no gables, 2 doors (20 sq ft) and 10 windows (15 sq ft) subtracted. The coverage figure is 350 sq ft per gallon for smooth siding; PaintCare’s Buy Right guide gives 300 to 400 as the usual range. Real houses are not square, so measure yours.

With gables

Add two gables, each as wide as the house (32 ft one-story, 22 ft two-story) and 6 ft tall at the peak: area ½ × width × rise each. That raises the paintable area to 1,265 sq ft (one story) or 1,732 sq ft (two stories), so two coats need 7.23 or 9.90 gallons.

Primer

On bare wood or heavy color changes, one primer coat at 250 sq ft per gallon is 4.30 gallons (one story): 1 five-gallon bucket, about $120 at typical primer prices.

FAQ

How many gallons of paint to paint a 1,000 sq ft house?

About 6.14 gallons for two coats if it is one story, 9.13 gallons if it is two stories (square footprint, no gables, smooth siding).

How much does it cost to paint the outside of a 1,000 sq ft house?

Paint alone is about $265 (one story) or $400 (two stories) for two coats at typical prices; labor and supplies are extra.

Does siding type matter for a 1,000 sq ft house?

Yes: stucco or brick at 200 sq ft per gallon needs 10.75 gallons for the one-story version versus 6.14 for smooth siding.
